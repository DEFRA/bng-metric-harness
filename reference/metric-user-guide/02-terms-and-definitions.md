## 2 Terms and definitions

See table 1 for an explanation of the terms used within this guidance.

**Table 1 Explanation of guidance terms**

| Tool term | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **Area habitat** | Habitats recorded in the biodiversity metric tool in area (hectares). |
| **Assessor** | The competent person completing the biodiversity metric tool. |
| **Biodiversity unit** | ‘Biodiversity units’ are used to describe relative biodiversity value. There are three types of biodiversity units: area habitat units, hedgerow units and watercourse units. Each of these are calculated in separate ‘modules’ of the biodiversity metric. |
| **BNG boundary** | The area of the development site of an NSIP that is subject to BNG and must be included in the baseline. This term only applies to NSIPs. |
| **Commercial building** | A building that is not used as a domestic dwelling. Uses could include industrial, retail, leisure, healthcare facilities as listed in The Town and Country Planning (Use Classes) (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2020, [2020 No. 757](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/757/contents). Regulation 13<br>[Find out more about commercial, business and service use classes.](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/757/regulation/13#:%7E:text=Class%20E.&text=being%20a%20use%2C%20which%20can,%2C%20ash%2C%20dust%20or%20grit.) |
| **Condition** | A measure of the habitat against its ecological optimum state. Condition is a way of measuring variation in the quality of patches of the same habitat type. |
| **Condition assessment** | The process of assigning habitat condition, to be undertaken by a competent person. |
| **Difficulty** | A measure which represents the uncertainty in the effectiveness of management techniques used to enhance or create habitat. |
| **Distinctiveness** | A measure based on the type of habitat and its distinguishing features. This includes:<br>• consideration of species richness and rarity<br>• the extent to which the habitat is protected by designations<br>• the degree to which a habitat supports species rarely found in other habitats. |
| **Relevant decision maker** | For development that needs planning permission under the Town and Country Planning Act 1990, the relevant decision maker is the relevant planning authority.<br>For NSIPs, the relevant decision maker will vary depending on the NSIP’s stage in the consenting process. For the BNG information submitted to the Planning Inspectorate as part of the development consent order application, the relevant decision maker is the Secretary of State. For any BNG information submitted for subsequent approval after development consent is granted, the decision maker is the discharging authority. This may be the relevant planning authority or the Secretary of State. |
| **Reviewer** | A person reviewing the biodiversity metric outputs to check the biodiversity metric rules, principles and guidance have been followed. A review is usually from a relevant planning authority.<br>For NSIPs, this can also be the Planning Inspectorate (Examining Authority) or the Secretary of State. |
| **Habitat type** | Users should see ‘habitat definitions’ tab in the Statutory Metric condition assessment sheets for definitive source classifications for each habitat.<br>The technical data sheets in the statutory biodiversity metric tool (G-1 All Habitats) also list the biodiversity metric habitat types and their source material.<br>Source material includes:<br>• UK Habitat Classification<br>• European Nature Information System habitat type hierarchical view<br>• Water Framework Directive Lake typologies<br>• Natura 2000 (Annex I habitats) |
| **Habitat parcel** | A linked area of habitat of the same distinctiveness, condition and strategic significance.<br>Find out more about combining habitat parcels in the tool troubleshooting section. |
| **Impact site or on-site** | The area within the red line boundary for developments that need planning permission, or the BNG boundary for NSIPs. |
| **Linear habitat** | Habitats recorded in the biodiversity metric according to length (kilometres) instead of area (hectares). This includes habitats in the hedgerow and watercourse modules. It’s taken as a centre line measurement along the length of the feature. |
| **Private garden** | A garden within the curtilage of a privately owned or tenanted dwellinghouse.<br>The built structures of a privately owned or tenanted dwellinghouse are not considered part of a private garden.<br>[Find out more about dwellinghouses.](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/764/schedule/made) |
| **Project timeframe** | The timeframe over which the biodiversity metric calculates gains and losses for specific habitat interventions. |
| **Size** | The size of the habitat parcel to be retained, enhanced, created, or lost. Size is measured in hectares for area features or in kilometres for linear features. The biodiversity metric tool accepts size measurements to any number of decimal places. |
| **Spatial risk** | Spatial risk represents the relationship between the location of biodiversity loss (on-site) and where the off-site habitat is being delivered. This is applied to off-site interventions only. |
| **Strategic significance** | The local significance of the habitat based on its location and the habitat type. |
| **Time to target condition** | The average time taken between starting creation or enhancement of habitats and that habitat reaching its target condition or distinctiveness. |

### On-site and off-site

On-site refers to all land within a red line boundary of a development that needs planning permission, or all land within a BNG boundary of an NSIP development.

Off-site, for the purposes of the biodiversity metric tool, refers to land outside of the on-site boundary, which is dedicated to habitat interventions (habitat enhancement or creation), regardless of proximity or ownership.

You should not adjust a project red line boundary to move habitats only subject to enhancement to off-site sections of the biodiversity metric tool.

[See more information about setting the red line boundary for developments that need planning permission.](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/making-an-application)

[For NSIPs, see more information about what to include in the BNG boundary and baseline](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/what-to-include-in-biodiversity-net-gain-baselines-for-nsips).

### Off-site providers

An off-site provider can create or enhance habitats to generate biodiversity units for developments and allow them to meet their BNG requirement. An off-site provider could be a land manager, a landowner, or an organisation acting as a habitat bank.

If you are an off-site provider completing an assessment of your land, you should complete the off-site sections of the biodiversity metric tool. You can select *'This metric is being used by an off-site provider’* as a spatial risk category before you have agreed to sell the units to a development.

When your off-site biodiversity units are used by a development, they will be subject to a spatial risk multiplier. If you are a development using an off-site provider, you must apply the correct spatial risk multiplier. The biodiversity metric tool will calculate the value of off-site actions relative to the project.

See more about the spatial risk multiplier and how it is applied.
